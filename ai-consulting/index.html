Human handoff

A good agent knows when to stop.

The fear most owners have is a customer stuck arguing with a machine. So every agent I build has a clear exit, and a person waiting on the other side of it.

Clear stop rules

The agent hands off when a request is outside its job, when it has tried and missed, when the topic is sensitive, or the moment someone asks for a person. Asking for a human always works the first time.

The whole story travels

Your staff receive what the customer said and what the agent already tried. Nobody has to repeat themselves.

It reaches the right person

Calls forward to your office phone number. Chats move to a live teammate. No new system for your team to learn.

The customer hears what is next

No silence and no dead ends. They are told who has it and what to expect.

Every handoff also shows what to teach the agent next. The common ones become things it can handle on its own.

AI fluency

Tools are easy to buy. Fluency is the part that pays.

AI fluency means working with AI in ways that are effective, efficient, ethical and safe. I teach your team four habits, so the skill still holds up when the tools change.

Habit 01

Delegation

Knowing what to hand over and what to keep. The front desk agent takes the hours question. You keep the tough call. Good delegation starts with knowing the job and what the tool can really do.

Habit 02

Description

Telling AI exactly what you want. Your tone, your policies, your idea of a great answer. Vague instructions in, vague work out. This is where most do it yourself setups fall short.

Habit 03

Discernment

Checking the work. Your team learns to spot a wrong or weak answer, push back on it and correct it. Don't take the first answer. Challenge it.

Habit 04

Diligence

Owning the result. Being honest about where AI is used, protecting customer information, and standing behind everything that goes out under your name.

How we get there

From first call to full handoff

  1. It starts with your core values

    We map how your business talks, what customers ask most, and what a great answer sounds like. We can use material you already have or start from scratch.

  2. I build the knowledge base

    General information, FAQs, prices and policies, organized so the agent finds the right answer and knows its limits.

  3. Test, push back, refine

    I challenge the agent with hard questions before a customer ever does. I don't take the first answer.

  4. Launch with a safety net

    We go live with the stop rules in place and a real person one step away.

  5. Hand off and train

    A complete handoff of the product, then hands on training so your staff can tweak, upgrade and reteach it with confidence.
